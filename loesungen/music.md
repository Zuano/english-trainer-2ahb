## 1. Lesetext „Moved by music“ (in der App satzweise mit Dekodierung)

Kernaussagen: 1) „Happy“ war ein Riesenhit, aber harte Arbeit (neun Versionen). 2) Musik setzt Dopamin frei (Studie aus Kanada). 3) Kaum ein Lied gefällt allen – Geschmackssache. 4) Musik steuert Stimmung und Verhalten (Filme, Supermärkte, Modegeschäfte). 5) Musik als Kontrolle: Nationalhymnen, Marschrhythmen, klassische Musik vertreibt Jugendliche („moved along“).

### Reading Ex. 2+3 – grüne Wörter → Definitionen
1. an excited or frightened feeling = **a shiver** (Schauer)
2. words of a song full of hope, happiness and good feelings = **upbeat lyrics** (fröhlicher Text)
3. an unpleasant, loud, high sound = **a screeching noise** (kreischendes Geräusch)
4. a pleasing and easy to remember song = **a catchy tune** (eingängige Melodie / Ohrwurm)

### Reading Ex. 5 – Meinung des Autors
**2 – „Happy“ is liked by most people.** („the nearest you will get to a piece of music which is universally popular is perhaps a song like Happy“)

### Reading Ex. 6 – Reihenfolge der Themen
1 **c** Why Happy is so popular → 2 **e** Research into the effect of music → 3 **a** Different musical tastes → 4 **d** Music in different places → 5 **b** Controlling people with music

### Reading Ex. 7 – True/False mit Textbeleg
1. Happy was an easy song to write. → **False** („creating Happy was by no means as effortless as the song sounds“)
2. We don't know why music is connected with survival. → **True** („It's not clear why music is good for survival“)
3. It is difficult to find a song that everybody likes. → **True** („there are very few songs that everybody likes“)
4. Relaxing music can encourage people to spend money. → **True** („Supermarkets often play relaxing music … research suggests that this can increase sales“)
5. In recent times, music has been used to make people feel patriotic. → **False** („National anthems have been used **for centuries** to stimulate feelings of patriotism“ – seit Jahrhunderten, nicht erst seit Kurzem)
6. Young people don't seem to like classical music. → **True** („there is nothing better than a little Mozart or opera to get rid of young people“)

### Reading Ex. 8 – Multiple Choice
1. Why did Pharrell Williams write so many versions of Happy? → **b** He wanted to get just the right version to move people.
2. What is the main point of the whole text? → **c** Music influences the way people feel and act.

## 3. Grammar: Relative Clauses (S. 110–111)

### Ex. 1 – Festival-Fotos
1 **which** (rock festival) · 2 **which** (bus) · 3 **who** (girls) · 4 **where** (place we ate) · 5 **when** (less busy) · 6 **where** (place we slept) · 7 **whose** (the girl whose tent it was)

### Ex. 2 – Ersetzbar durch „that“: Nr. 2 (which) und Nr. 3 (who). where/when/whose können NICHT durch that ersetzt werden.

### Ex. 4 – Tabellen-Sätze
1. A composer is someone **who/that writes musical scores**.
2. A microphone is something **which/that is used for recording sounds**.
3. A studio is a room **where musicians record songs**.
4. Late morning is the time **when the festival site is quietest**.
5. The main act is the band **whose music is the most popular at the moment**.

### Ex. 5 – Sätze verbinden
1. Happy is a song **which/that** almost everybody likes.
2. A hit is a piece of music **which/that** is very popular.
3. I've got a neighbour **who/that** has just taken up the violin.
4. You must find a piece of music **(which/that)** everybody likes.
5. It's the kind of music **(which/that)** I hate.
6. Music can release something in the brain **which/that** makes people feel good.
7. Play us a song **(which/that)** we can all sing along to.
8. The lead guitarist is the one **who/that** plays most of the solos.

### Ex. 7 – Weglassbare Pronomen
Nur Objekt-Pronomen dürfen wegfallen: in den Sätzen **1, 4, 5, 7** (z. B. „Happy is a song almost everybody likes.“). In 2, 3, 6, 8 ist das Pronomen Subjekt → muss bleiben.

### Ex. 8 – Relativsätze in den Text einsetzen (Reihenfolge war vorgegeben)
The style of music **I'm most into** is heavy rock. I like the old bands **most people have heard of**, such as AC/DC and Metallica. But I also like new stuff. For example, a band **I listen to a lot at the moment** is called Halestorm. The lead singer is a girl **whose surname is Hale**, and the guy **who plays drums** is her brother. They play songs **which are fun and full of energy**. People **who don't like this kind of music** think heavy rock fans are angry and violent, but it's not true – the ones **I know** are all very calm and gentle.

### Ex. 11 – Non-defining relative clauses
1. Satz **a** braucht Kommas (Pharrell Williams, who …, knew …). 2. Vor „who“ und nach „years“. 3. Weil der Relativsatz nur Zusatzinformation ist (Pharrell Williams ist eindeutig). 4. Nur in Satz **b** (defining) kann who durch that ersetzt werden – in non-defining Sätzen ist **that verboten**.

### Ex. 12 – Non-defining einbauen
1. Mr Jones, who was my music teacher, plays in a jazz band.
2. The FIB festival, which takes place in Benicàssim, is great fun.
3. My mum, who's a great singer, loves The Beatles.
4. My mum loves The Beatles, who were a British pop group.
5. Mozart, who was born in Austria, was a great composer.
6. The cello, which is bigger than a violin, has a very deep sound.
7. The smallest instrument is the piccolo, which is similar to a flute.
8. The most famous reggae singer was Bob Marley, whose home was in Jamaica.
9. Wembley, which is a football stadium, is often used for massive concerts.
10. I've just come back from Salamanca, where my grandmother lives.
