**Kurzübersicht der Typen:**

| Typ | Verwendung | if-Satz | Hauptsatz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | immer wahr (Naturgesetze, Regeln) | Present Simple | Present Simple |
| I | real / erfüllbar (Zukunft) | Present Simple | will + Infinitiv (auch can/must/should, Imperativ) |
| II | bloße Annahme, unwahrscheinlich (Gegenwart/Zukunft) | Past Simple (be → **were**) | would/could/might + Infinitiv |
| III | Vergangenheit, nicht passiert (unmöglich) | Past Perfect | would/could/might **have** + Past Participle |

**Merkregeln (aus den Blättern):** Kein *will* und kein *would* im if-Satz! · Der if-Satz kann vorne (mit Komma) oder hinten (ohne Komma) stehen. · *unless* = *if not*. · *when* statt *if*, wenn etwas sicher passiert. · *'d* = *would* oder *had*; statt *had had* meist *'d had*.

## 1 | Conditionals 0,1,2 – Forms and Structures

### Type 0 – "Form meaningful sentences" (Übung, Nr. 1–8)

Modellsatz: *You travel in the sun for hours. You get sunburnt quite easily.* → **If you travel in the sun for hours, you get sunburnt quite easily.**

1. **If you stop eating, you starve.**
   → Typ 0: allgemein gültige Tatsache (gilt immer) → beide Teile Present Simple.
2. **If there is a lot of snow, driving conditions are dangerous.**
   → Typ 0: immer wahre Folge → Present Simple + Present Simple.
3. **If people eat too much junk food, they gain weight.**
   → Typ 0: gilt grundsätzlich für alle → Present Simple in beiden Teilen.
4. **If a serious hurricane hits Florida, the inhabitants leave the area.**
   → Typ 0: typischer, sich wiederholender Ablauf → Present Simple (hits mit 3.-Person-s!).
5. **If I regularly watch the news in English, my English improves a lot.**
   → Typ 0: regelmäßige, immer gültige Folge → Present Simple + Present Simple.
6. **If elderly people have a pet, they are happier.**
   → Typ 0: allgemeine Aussage → Present Simple + Present Simple.
7. **If we do a lot of sports, we are in good shape.**
   → Typ 0: immer wahr → Present Simple + Present Simple.
8. **If you grow up in China, you learn to speak Chinese.**
   → Typ 0: gilt grundsätzlich → Present Simple + Present Simple.

### Type 1 – Your Turn 1 (Nr. 1–10)

Alle Sätze: **Typ I** – reale, erfüllbare Bedingung in Gegenwart/Zukunft → *if + Present Simple, Hauptsatz will + Infinitiv* (oder Modalverb).

1. If I **wake up** late on Monday, I **will miss** the bus.
   → Typ I: realistische Zukunftsbedingung; kein will im if-Satz.
2. If you **move** to Cuba, you **must speak** Spanish.
   → Typ I: Hauptsatz hier mit Modalverb *must* statt will.
3. If you **learn** hard, you **will pass** your maths exam.
   → Typ I: erfüllbare Bedingung → Present Simple + will.
4. If you **eat** healthy food, you **will become** quite old.
   → Typ I: mögliche Zukunftsfolge → Present Simple + will.
5. If you **do not hurry up**, you **will miss** your plane.
   → Typ I: Verneinung im if-Satz mit *do not* (Present Simple).
6. If you **study** English three times a week, you **will improve** quickly.
   → Typ I: reale Möglichkeit → Present Simple + will.
7. If it **rains** this weekend, we **will not go** on holiday to Hungary.
   → Typ I: Hauptsatz verneint mit *will not*.
8. If you **are** too busy, I **will not come** round for a cup of coffee.
   → Typ I: be im if-Satz als Present Simple (*are*), Hauptsatz will not.
9. If you **do not leave** my flat at once, I **will call** the police.
   → Typ I: Drohung/reale Folge in der Zukunft.
10. If she **offers** me the job of personal assistant, I **will accept** this new opportunity.
    → Typ I: offers mit 3.-Person-s; Hauptsatz will + Infinitiv.

### Type 2 – Your Turn 1 (Nr. 1–10)

Alle Sätze: **Typ II** – bloße Annahme / unwahrscheinliche Möglichkeit → *if + Past Simple, Hauptsatz would/could + Infinitiv*; **be → were** im if-Satz.

1. If I **were** ten years younger, I **would emigrate** to Australia.
   → Typ II: Annahme (ich bin nicht jünger); be → were.
2. We are always late! If we **left** punctually at six o'clock, we **would not have** any stress catching the early morning train.
   → Typ II: Gegenteil der Realität (wir fahren nie pünktlich los) → Past Simple + would.
3. If I **were** a politician, I **would fight** for free health care and education.
   → Typ II: unwahrscheinliche Annahme; were statt was (Regel vom Blatt).
4. If you **drank** too much alcohol, you **would feel** sleepy quite easily.
   → Typ II: gedachte Situation → drank (Past Simple) + would feel.
5. If you **had** a spanner, you **could fix** your bike.
   → Typ II: Hauptsatz mit Modalverb *could* (= könntest) statt would.
6. If we **had** more money, we **would invest** most of it in schools.
   → Typ II: Annahme (wir haben das Geld nicht) → had + would invest.
7. If I **spoke** Japanese fluently, I **would travel** to Japan.
   → Typ II: nicht reale Gegenwart → spoke (Past Simple) + would travel.
8. If I **were** slimmer, I **would buy** designer clothes.
   → Typ II: Annahme über die Gegenwart; be → were.
9. If I **had** more time and money, I **could stop** working and **go** abroad.
   → Typ II: Hauptsatz mit could; das zweite Verb (*go*) hängt am selben could.
10. If I **were** you, I **would buy** that new car.
    → Typ II: feste Ratschlag-Wendung *If I were you* + would.

> ⚠️ Im Scan ist nach dem Typ-2-Teil eine Überschrift „Your Turn 2" erkennbar, der zugehörige Aufgabentext fehlt aber (Seite abgeschnitten). Falls dieses Übungsteil im Original-Heft existiert, bitte am Papier nachschauen.

### Konjunktionen-Box (Lernstoff, keine Aufgabe)

if = wenn/falls · unless = wenn nicht/außer wenn · in case = für den Fall, dass · on condition that = unter der Bedingung, dass · provided/providing (that) = vorausgesetzt, dass · supposing (that) = angenommen, dass · as long as = solange/vorausgesetzt, dass.
Komma nur, wenn der if-Satz zuerst steht: *If he comes, I will tell him.* ABER *I will tell him if he comes.*

## 2 | Conditionals Explained (Präsentation)

Regelübersicht – Beispielsätze mit Typ-Begründung:

- *If you heat water, it boils.* → **Typ 0**: Naturgesetz, Present + Present.
- *If you want to study in the USA, you have to pass an exam in English.* → **Typ 0**: feste Regel.
- *If you go to the Zoo, you will see the lions.* → **Typ I**: reale Zukunft, Present + will.
- *If I pass my exams, my parents will buy me an iPhone X.* → **Typ I**: mögliches Zukunftsereignis.
- *If we save enough money, we can go on holiday.* → **Typ I** mit can.
- *If you see him, tell him to give me a ring.* → **Typ I** mit Imperativ im Hauptsatz.
- *If I had enough money, I would buy a new car.* → **Typ II**: unwahrscheinlich/vorgestellt, Past + would.
- *If I were you, I would study more.* → **Typ II**: unmögliche Gegenwart (ich bin nicht du).
- *If I had studied more, I would have passed the exam.* → **Typ III**: Vergangenheit, nicht passiert.
- *If I hadn't broken my leg, I would have gone skiing.* → **Typ III**: unreale Vergangenheit.
- *You don't get fit unless you exercise regularly.* = *You don't get fit if you don't exercise regularly.* → **unless = if not** (Satz selbst: Typ 0).
- *I won't go there unless I take a taxi.* = *I won't go there if I don't take a taxi.* → unless = if not (Satz selbst: Typ I).

### "Finish these sentences" (offene Aufgabe – Beispiellösungen)

1. If I pass my Analytical Chemistry exam, **I will celebrate with my friends.**
   → Typ I: Present Simple im if-Satz → Hauptsatz mit will + Infinitiv.
2. If I passed my Analytical Chemistry exam, **I would be really happy.**
   → Typ II: Past Simple im if-Satz → Hauptsatz mit would + Infinitiv.
3. If I had passed my Analytical Chemistry exam, **I would have got my degree.**
   → Typ III: Past Perfect im if-Satz → Hauptsatz mit would have + Past Participle.

## 3 | First and second conditional (Linguahouse, Code 4ARL-E7IF-964V)

> ⚠️ Im Scan war nur die Presentation-Seite (1/3) enthalten – die Übungsseiten 2–3 dieses Blattes fehlen. Falls sie am Papier vorhanden sind: Die Web-App-Übung `cond_ex04` deckt die Beispielsätze der Presentation ab.

Beispielsätze mit Typ-Begründung:

- *If it rains, I'll stay at home.* → **Typ I**: echte Möglichkeit (Wetterbericht sagt Regen an) → Present + will.
- *If I don't call you tonight, you can call me.* → **Typ I**: statt will auch can/might/may/must/should.
- *Will you wait for me if I am late?* → **Typ I** als Frage: will nur im Hauptsatz.
- *If it was sunny, I'd go out.* → **Typ II**: Gegenteil der Realität (es regnet gerade) → Past + would. (Blatt schreibt *was*; die Schulregel vom anderen Blatt erlaubt/bevorzugt *were*.)
- *What would you do if you found a lot of money in the street?* → **Typ II**: vorgestellte Situation; Past Simple bedeutet hier nichts Vergangenes.
- *If it wasn't dark outside, we could go out.* → **Typ II** mit could.
- *When she comes, I'll call you.* → **when statt if**, weil es sicher passiert (Struktur wie Typ I).

Merkregeln: Kein *will/would* im if-Teil (~~If it will rain~~ → If it **rains**) · if-Teil vorne oder hinten möglich.

## 4 | Third conditional (Linguahouse/Expemo, Code 1J11-S1L5-128 – TEACHER-Version, Key verbindlich)

### Ex. 1 – Warm up

Satz: *They would have arrived on time if they had not missed the bus.*
→ **Typ III**: Hauptsatz would have + Past Participle (*arrived*), if-Satz Past Perfect (*had not missed*) – unreale Vergangenheit.

1. What event caused them to be late? → **They missed the bus.**
2. What was the result of them missing the bus? → **They did not arrive on time.**
3. How would the situation have been different if they had caught the bus? → **They would have arrived on time.**
4. What could they have done differently? → **They could have left home earlier or checked the bus schedule.**

### Ex. 2 Part B – Lücken füllen (Nr. 1–8, Lösungen = Teacher-Key)

Alle Sätze: **Typ III** – unreale Vergangenheit → *if + Past Perfect, Hauptsatz would have + Past Participle*.

1. If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed** the exam.
   → Typ III: Realität – ich habe nicht hart genug gelernt und bin durchgefallen.
2. They **wouldn't have gotten** lost if they **had followed** the map.
   → Typ III: Hauptsatz vorne; *gotten* = amerikanisches Past Participle von get (so im Key; britisch wäre *got*).
3. If she **had asked** for help, someone **would have assisted** her.
   → Typ III: sie hat nicht um Hilfe gebeten → if + Past Perfect.
4. We **would have attended** the concert if we **had bought** tickets earlier.
   → Typ III: if-Satz hinten → kein Komma.
5. If it **hadn't rained** yesterday, we **would have gone** for a picnic.
   → Typ III: Signalwort *yesterday*; verneintes Past Perfect (*hadn't rained*).
6. He **would have been** on time if his alarm clock **hadn't broken** down.
   → Typ III: would have been im Hauptsatz, hadn't broken down im if-Satz.
7. If they **hadn't missed** the bus, they **would have arrived** on time.
   → Typ III: tatsächlich haben sie den Bus verpasst.
8. She **would have felt** better if she **had taken** the medicine earlier.
   → Typ III: had taken (Past Perfect) im if-Satz.

### Ex. 2 Part C – Nicks Probleme (Nr. 1–6, Lösungen = Teacher-Key)

Ideen-Box: change the oil / cook his lunch properly / do some shopping / have an umbrella / keep a diary / pay his internet bill. Alle Sätze **Typ III** (gestern passiert, nicht mehr änderbar).

1. He was not able to use the internet. → **If he had paid his internet bill, he would have been able to use the internet.**
   → Typ III: if + Past Perfect (had paid), Hauptsatz would have been.
2. He forgot his best friend's birthday. → **If he had kept a diary, he would have remembered his best friend's birthday.**
   → Typ III: had kept + would have remembered.
3. His car broke down. → **His car wouldn't have broken down if he had changed the oil.**
   → Typ III: Hauptsatz (verneint) vorne, if-Satz hinten ohne Komma.
4. He ran out of coffee. → **If he had done some shopping, he wouldn't have run out of coffee.**
   → Typ III: had done + wouldn't have run.
5. He was soaking wet. → **If he'd had an umbrella, he wouldn't have been soaking wet.**
   → Typ III: *'d had* = *had had* (Past Perfect von have).
6. He got food poisoning in the evening. → **If he had cooked his lunch properly, he wouldn't have got food poisoning in the evening.**
   → Typ III: had cooked + wouldn't have got.

### Ex. 3 Part A – Reading "The Missed Opportunity" (Nr. 1–4, Key)

1. **b** – They planned to go to a popular concert.
2. **b** – She overslept because her alarm didn't go off.
3. **b** – disappointed and frustrated.
4. **a** – She should check her alarm and leave earlier to avoid missing important events.

### Ex. 3 Part B – Sätze zum Text vervollständigen (Nr. 1–4, Lösungen = Teacher-Key)

Alle Sätze **Typ III** (Emmas Tag ist vorbei, nichts mehr änderbar).

1. If Emma **had checked** her alarm the night before, she **would have woken up** on time.
   → Typ III: had checked + would have woken up.
2. If Emma **had woken up** on time, she **would have caught** the bus.
   → Typ III: had woken up + would have caught.
3. If Emma **had caught** the bus, she **would have arrived** at the concert on time (with her friends).
   → Typ III: had caught + would have arrived.
4. If Emma **had set** her alarm correctly, she **would not have missed** the concert.
   → Typ III: had set + would not have missed (verneint).

### Ex. 4 Speaking practice & Ex. 5 Talking point (offene Sprechaufgaben – Beispielantworten)

Eigene Geschichte + Diskussionsfragen, jeweils im Third Conditional. Beispielantworten:

1. What would you have done if you had won the lottery last year? → **If I had won the lottery last year, I would have bought a house for my family.** (Typ III)
2. If you had studied harder for your last exam, how would your results have changed? → **If I had studied harder, I would have got a much better grade.** (Typ III)
3. Where would you have travelled if you had had a month off last summer? → **If I'd had a month off, I would have travelled to Japan.** (Typ III, 'd had = had had)
4. What would have happened if you had missed the bus to school yesterday? → **If I had missed the bus yesterday, I would have been late for the first lesson.** (Typ III)
5. How would your life be different now if you had moved to a different city five years ago? → **If I had moved to a different city five years ago, I would not live near my friends now.** (⚠️ Mixed Conditional: if-Satz Typ III [Vergangenheit], Hauptsatz Typ II [Folge in der Gegenwart, *now*] – so von der Frage vorgegeben, für die Schularbeit nur als Bonus wissen.)
