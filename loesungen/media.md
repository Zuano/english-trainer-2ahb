## 1. Reading: „Traditional Media and New Media" – Fragen zum Text

**1. Give two differences between traditional and new media.**

> Two differences are the **lead time** and the **commitment to quality**. Traditional media needs more time to prepare news because journalists research, write and check facts, while new media publishes immediately. Traditional media also feels responsible for correct and reliable information, while new media is less controlled.

Begründung: Der Text nennt insgesamt **vier** Unterschiede – lead time (Absatz 2), commitment to quality (Absatz 3), cumulative effect (Absatz 4), instant and interactive (Absatz 5). Zwei beliebige davon mit je einem Erklärungssatz reichen.

**2. What does "lead time" mean in the text?**

> "Lead time" means the time media need to **prepare news before publishing**. Journalists research their topics, write articles and check the facts, so it can take hours or even a whole day before the news reaches the audience.

**3. Why does traditional media often have greater longevity?**

> Because traditional media companies feel responsible for giving **correct and reliable information**, people **trust their reports over time** – so the reports stay relevant and trusted longer.

**4. Do you think new media is always reliable? Explain your answer.** *(Meinungsfrage – Musterantwort)*

> No, I don't think so. New media is often **less controlled** – anyone can publish content, so some information might not be true or complete. Because information is shared so quickly, it is sometimes not checked properly. That is why I always try to compare different sources and think critically.

## 2. fake news.pdf

### Aufgabe 1: Fake News Discussion *(offene Fragen – Beispielantworten auf B1-Niveau)*

1. **What is fake news?** – Fake news is false or misleading information that is presented as if it were real news. It is often spread on social media.
2. **How do you know if the news you read is true?** – I check the source, compare several credible media outlets and look for evidence. If a story sounds too shocking, I factcheck it before I believe it.
3. **Have you seen any fake news? / in your country?** – Yes, for example false health tips or fake quotes by celebrities on social media. In Austria, false stories about politicians sometimes spread on WhatsApp and Facebook before they are corrected.
4. **Why do people believe fake news?** – Because of **confirmation bias**: people prefer information that confirms what they already believe. Fake news is also emotional and often looks professional.
5. **Who is responsible for creating fake news? What is the purpose?** – Trolls, some political groups and people who earn money with clicks. The purpose is to influence opinions, win elections or make money (clickbait).
6. **Can we believe anything in the news?** – We can trust credible media more because they check their facts, but we should always think critically and compare sources.
7. **Why does fake news spread faster than real news?** – Because it is sensational and emotional, people share it quickly without checking it. Algorithms then show popular posts to even more people.
8. **What effect does fake news have on society?** – It creates distrust in the media, divides society and can influence opinions and even elections.

### Fake News vocabulary comprehension questions *(persönliche Fragen – Beispielantworten)*

1. *Confirmation bias?* – Probably yes. Everyone tends to click on articles that match their own opinion. I try to read different sources to avoid it.
2. *Misinformation/disinformation gesehen?* – Yes, e.g. wrong health tips that people shared by mistake (misinformation) and targeted political lies before elections (disinformation).
3. *Rise of post-truth politics?* – Social media rewards emotional content, so simple emotional messages work better than complicated facts.
4. *Propaganda in your country?* – For example one-sided election posters and ads that present one party as the only solution to every problem.
5. *Favourite satires?* – For example „Die Tagespresse" (an Austrian satirical news site) or late-night comedy shows.
6. *How can you factcheck?* – Check the original source and the date, compare credible media, use fact-checking sites (e.g. Mimikama, APA-Faktencheck). Honestly, I don't always do it before sharing – but I should!

### Aufgabe 4: Fake News Conversation Questions *(Beispielantworten)*

1. *Fake news vs. opinion?* – An opinion is clearly someone's personal view. Fake news pretends to be a fact although it is false.
2. *Do people believe anything on the internet?* – Many do, because posts look professional and friends share them. Confirmation bias makes it worse.
3. *Did fake news exist before social media?* – Yes – propaganda in wars or invented newspaper stories existed long before. Social media only makes it spread much faster.
4. *Why might someone call real news fake news?* – To attack journalists they don't like and to make people distrust critical reports about them.
5. *Distrust of mainstream media?* – Yes, partly. If people trust nothing, they often believe even more disinformation – this divides society.
6. *Can media be biased and still tell the truth?* – Yes. A report can contain true facts but present them one-sidedly (selection of facts, emotional language).
7. *What can be done against fake news?* – Media literacy at school, fact-checkers on platforms, checking sources before sharing, reporting false posts.
8. *Should fake news be illegal?* – Difficult: a ban could protect society, but it could also limit free speech, and someone would have to decide what is „true". Better: education and transparent fact-checking.

### Aufgabe 5: Fake News Writing Task *(Mustertext, ca. 115 Wörter)*

> Fake news is one of the biggest problems of social media. In my opinion, it is created for two main reasons: to make money with clickbait and to manipulate people's opinions, for example before elections. The effect on society is serious. Because of confirmation bias, many people believe disinformation, and this leads to distrust of the media and even of democracy. So what can we do? First, we should factcheck information with credible sources before sharing it. Second, schools should teach media literacy and critical thinking. Finally, platforms need more fact-checkers to flag false posts. Summing up, fake news will not disappear, but if we all think critically, it loses its power.

Verwendete Vokabeln: clickbait, confirmation bias, disinformation, factcheck, credible, fact-checkers, critical thinking. ✓
